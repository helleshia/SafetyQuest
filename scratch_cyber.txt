Module 10: Cyberbullying & Online Safety
1. INTRODUCTION
Brief Introduction to the Topic
The internet has become an important part of everyday life. Learners use smartphones,
computers, tablets, social media, online games, messaging applications, and educational
platforms to communicate, study, create content, and have fun. These technologies provide
many benefits, but they can also expose young people to risks such as cyberbullying, online
harassment, scams, inappropriate content, privacy violations, impersonation, and unwanted
contact from other people. Cyberbullying refers to harmful or repeated behavior carried out
through digital technologies with the intention or effect of hurting, embarrassing,
threatening, humiliating, or excluding another person. It can happen through social media
posts, comments, private messages, group chats, online games, videos, photos, or other
digital platforms.
Online safety means using digital technology in ways that protect one's personal
information, privacy, physical and emotional well-being, accounts, and relationships with
others.
Cyberbullying and online safety are important because something posted online can spread
quickly, be copied, saved, or shared with many people. A harmful message may take only a
few seconds to create but can have a lasting effect on the person who receives it.
Why the Topic Is Important
Learning about cyberbullying and online safety helps learners:
§ recognize unsafe or harmful online behavior;
§ protect their personal information;
§ develop responsible digital habits;
§ respond appropriately when cyberbullying happens;
§ avoid engaging with online conGlicts;
§ identify trusted adults who can provide help;
§ understand that online actions can affect real people;
§ know when to block, report, save evidence, or seek help;
§ communicate respectfully even when they disagree with others.
It is also important to remember that being bullied online is not the victim's fault.
Learners should not feel that they have to solve serious online problems alone.
Age-Appropriate Explanation for Learners
Think of the internet as a very large community. Just as we are expected to behave
respectfully at school, at home, or in public places, we should also behave respectfully online.
For example, if someone sends you a mean message, posts an embarrassing picture of you,
repeatedly excludes you from online groups, or threatens you through a messaging
application, that behavior should not simply be ignored because it happened online.
At the same time, not every disagreement online is cyberbullying. People can have different
opinions, misunderstand each other, or have an argument. Cyberbullying generally involves
harmful behaviour such as repeated harassment, humiliation, threats, or deliberate
targeting. Regardless of the label, however, behavior that makes you feel unsafe or
threatened deserves attention and support.
Remember: Behind every username, profile, photo, and account is a real person. Think
before you post, send, comment, or share.
2. Learning Objectives
At the end of the module, learners should be able to:
Objective 1: Define cyberbullying and distinguish it from ordinary online disagreements,
jokes, or misunderstandings.
Objective 2: Identify common forms of cyberbullying and other online safety risks,
including harassment, impersonation, unwanted contact, privacy violations, and harmful
content.
Objective 3: Demonstrate appropriate strategies for staying safe online, including
protecting personal information, using privacy settings, blocking or reporting harmful
accounts, and seeking help from trusted adults.
Objective 4: Apply the STOP–SAVE–BLOCK/REPORT–TELL strategy when experiencing or
witnessing cyberbullying or another serious online safety concern.
3. Interactive Lesson
A. Understanding Cyberbullying
What Is Cyberbullying?
Cyberbullying is harmful behavior that occurs through digital technologies. It may involve:
• sending repeated hurtful messages;
• posting insulting or humiliating comments;
• spreading rumors online;
• deliberately excluding someone from digital groups;
• sharing embarrassing images or videos without permission;
• creating fake accounts to embarrass or impersonate someone;
• threatening someone through messages;
• encouraging others to attack or ridicule someone online;
• repeatedly targeting someone through online games or group chats.
Cyberbullying can happen on:
social media;
messaging applications;
online games;
discussion boards;
video-sharing platforms;
livestreaming platforms;
school-related digital platforms;
email;
group chats.
Important Concept: Online Harm Is Still Real Harm
Sometimes people say:
“It's only online.”
This is not a safe way to think about harmful online behavior.
Online experiences can affect a person's:
emotions;
confidence;
relationships;
concentration;
school participation;
sense of safety;
willingness to participate online.
A digital message can also be viewed by many people and may be difficult to completely
remove once it has been shared.
B. Cyberbullying vs. Online Conflict
Not every negative online interaction is cyberbullying.
Example 1: Online disagreement
Two classmates disagree about the rules of an online game.
Possible situation: They express different opinions but do not threaten, repeatedly harass,
or humiliate each other.
This may be an online conflict or disagreement, rather than cyberbullying.
Example 2: Repeated harassment
A learner repeatedly receives insulting messages from the same group of classmates. The
messages continue even after the learner asks them to stop.
This is a serious online safety concern and may constitute cyberbullying.
Example 3: Embarrassing post
Someone secretly takes an embarrassing photo of a classmate and posts it in a group chat
so other students can laugh at the person.
This is harmful online behavior and should be addressed.
Important Reminder
The exact legal or school definition of cyberbullying may differ depending on the
jurisdiction, school policy, and circumstances. Learners do not need to determine the legal
definition themselves before asking for help.
If an online interaction makes you feel threatened, unsafe, seriously humiliated, or
repeatedly targeted, tell a trusted adult.
C. Common Forms of Cyberbullying
1. Hurtful Messages
Someone repeatedly sends insults, threats, or humiliating messages.
Example: “Nobody likes you. You should leave the group.”
2. Rumor Spreading
False or harmful information is deliberately posted or shared about another person.
Example: A student creates a group chat and encourages others to spread a false story about
a classmate.
3. Public Humiliation
Someone posts embarrassing information, images, or videos to make another person feel
ashamed.
4. Online Exclusion
A person is deliberately excluded from an online group or activity to embarrass or isolate
them.
5. Impersonation
Someone creates an account pretending to be another person.
Example:
A fake account uses a student's name and photo and posts inappropriate messages.
6. Threats
Someone sends messages threatening to hurt, expose, or harm another person.
Important: Threats should always be taken seriously and reported to a trusted adult.
7. Sharing Private Information
Someone shares another person's private information without permission.
Examples may include:
private conversations;
personal photographs;
home or school information;
phone numbers;
passwords;
private videos.
D. Other Online Safety Risks
Cyberbullying is only one online safety concern.
Learners should also be aware of:
Oversharing
Posting too much personal information online.
Examples:
full name;
home address;
phone number;
passwords;
school schedule;
live location;
private family information.
Fake Accounts and Impersonation
Not every account is operated by the person it claims to represent.
Online Scams

free prizes;
game rewards;
money;
