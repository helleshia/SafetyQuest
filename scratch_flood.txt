Module 4: Flood Safety
1. INTRODUCTION
Brief Introduction to the Topic
Flooding is one of the most common hazards experienced in the Philippines. It may occur
because of prolonged or intense rainfall, overflowing rivers and waterways, tropical
cyclones, drainage problems, dam releases, storm-related conditions, or a combination
of these factors.
A flood occurs when water covers land that is normally dry. Flooding can happen
gradually, giving people time to prepare, or it can happen very quickly as a flash flood,
leaving little time to respond.
Floodwater may damage homes, roads, schools, businesses, electrical systems,
vehicles, crops, and other property. More importantly, floodwater can threaten human
life. It may contain sewage, chemicals, sharp objects, debris, animals, or other
contaminants. Moving water can also be powerful enough to knock people down, sweep
away vehicles, or damage bridges and structures.
Flood safety therefore involves more than knowing how to respond once water has
entered a building. It includes:
o understanding flood hazards;
o monitoring weather and flood warnings;
o preparing before flooding occurs;
o knowing when and where to evacuate;
o protecting people and important belongings;
o avoiding dangerous floodwater;
o maintaining personal hygiene and safe drinking water; and
o safely returning home after authorities determine that conditions are safe.
DOST-PAGASA maintains flood forecasting and warning services and provides flood
bulletins, advisories, and other information to help communities take appropriate
precautionary measures.
Why Flood Safety Is Important
Flooding can change the conditions of a community very quickly. A road that is normally
safe may become impassable. A shallow-looking stream may become dangerous
because of strong currents. Electricity may become a serious hazard when electrical
equipment or lines encounter water.
This means that learners must understand an important principle:
Floodwater is not simply “water on the road.” It can contain multiple hazards that
are difficult to see.
Flood safety education helps learners:
§ recognize areas that may be prone to flooding;
§ understand flood warnings;
§ prepare an emergency kit;
§ identify evacuation routes;
§ know when to move to higher ground;
§ avoid walking or playing in floodwater;
§ understand electrical hazards during floods;
§ protect drinking water and food;
§ respond appropriately to evacuation instructions;
§ avoid unnecessary travel during flooding; and
§ safely return to an affected area after authorities declare it safe.
PAGASA emphasizes that people in flood-prone areas should know their
community's warning system, monitor weather conditions, identify evacuation
areas, prepare emergency supplies, and move to safe areas when necessary.
Age-Appropriate Explanation for Learners
For younger learners, flood safety can be explained simply:
A flood happens when too much water covers places that are normally dry. Floodwater
can be dangerous, so we should never play in it or enter it without a very good reason.
When adults or authorities tell us to evacuate, we should follow them and move to a
safe, higher place.
For older learners:
Flood safety means recognizing flood risks, preparing before flooding occurs,
responding appropriately to warnings, evacuating when necessary, avoiding
contaminated or moving water, and taking precautions when returning after the
flood.
Learners should remember:
WATER + SPEED + UNKNOWN HAZARDS = DANGER
Even water that appears shallow can be unsafe when it is moving quickly or
contaminated.

At the end of this module, learners should be able to:
Objective 1

Objective 2

Objective 3

3. INTERACTIVE LESSON
A. Understanding Floods
A flood occurs when water accumulates and covers land that is normally dry.
Flooding may occur because of:
o heavy or prolonged rainfall;
o overflowing rivers;
o overflowing creeks and streams;
o tropical cyclones;
o storm-related rainfall;
o inadequate drainage;
o rising water levels;
o dam releases;
o coastal or storm-related flooding; and
o other environmental or human-related factors.
The severity of a flood depends on many factors, including the amount and intensity of
rainfall, terrain, drainage, river conditions, land use, and the location of homes and
communities.
B. Types of Flooding
1. Gradual Flooding
Gradual flooding develops over a longer period.
For example: Several days of heavy rainfall cause the water level of a river to rise
continuously until the river overflows.
Because gradual flooding may provide more warning time, people may have more
opportunity to prepare and evacuate.
2. Flash Flooding
A flash flood can develop rapidly following intense rainfall or other conditions
that cause water to rise quickly.
Flash floods are particularly dangerous because:
o water levels can rise rapidly;
o roads may become impassable;
o bridges may become unsafe;
o people may have little time to react; and
o moving water can carry debris.
Learner Reminder
Never wait until floodwater is already surrounding your home before deciding
whether you need to evacuate.
Preparedness should happen before conditions become dangerous.
C. Understanding Flood Warnings
One of the most important parts of flood safety is paying attention to official
warnings.
PAGASA provides flood forecasting and warning information. Its flood warning
system includes different levels that correspond to changing flood conditions
and recommended actions.
A simplified way to understand the progression is:
MONITOR
Flooding is possible.
Action:
Stay informed.
Monitor weather and flood information.
Review your emergency plan.
ALERT
Flooding is becoming a threat.
Action:
Prepare to act.
Monitor official updates closely.
Prepare emergency supplies.
Be ready to evacuate.
WARNING / EMERGENCY
Flooding is occurring or severe flooding is expected.
Action:
Follow official instructions.
Evacuate when instructed.
Move to a safe location.
Avoid dangerous floodwater.
PAGASA's current warning framework identifies flood monitoring, flood alert,
flood warning, and severe flood warning conditions, with increasingly urgent
actions as the hazard develops.
Important Principle
A warning is useful only if people act on it.
Do not wait until the water reaches your home before taking action if authorities
have already instructed residents to evacuate.
BEFORE A FLOOD: PREPARE
Flood safety begins before the rain becomes dangerous.
1. Know Your Flood Risk
Ask: Does the area flood frequently?
Is the house near a river, creek, canal, or other waterway?
Are roads easily flooded?
Where is the nearest evacuation center?
Which routes remain accessible during floods?
Where is the nearest higher ground?
PAGASA provides flood-related information and flood hazard maps that can help
communities understand flood risks.
E. Know Your Community Warning System
Learners should know how their community receives emergency information.
Possible sources include:
PAGASA updates;
local government announcements;
barangay announcements;
emergency alerts;
television;
radio;
official social media channels; and
authorized emergency personnel.
Do not rely only on rumors or unverified social media posts.
Remember: Listen to official warnings. Verify before sharing. Act when instructed.
