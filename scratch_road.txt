Module 11: Road & Traffic Safety
1. INTRODUCTION
Brief Introduction to the Topic
Roads and streets are very important parts of everyday life. People use them to walk to
school, ride bicycles, travel in cars, ride motorcycles, take buses or jeepneys, and cross
streets. Because many people and vehicles share roads, everyone has a big responsibility to
use them safely. Road and traffic safety refers to the knowledge, skills, attitudes, and
behaviors that help prevent road crashes and reduce the risk of injury or death. It includes
knowing traffic rules, understanding road signs and signals, crossing roads safely, using
pedestrian crossings, wearing appropriate protective equipment, and behaving responsibly
as a pedestrian, passenger, cyclist, or other road user.
Road safety is not only the responsibility of drivers. Pedestrians, passengers, cyclists,
motorcycle riders, and other road users also play an important role in keeping roads safe.
Why the Topic Is Important
Road crashes can happen when people:
o cross without looking;
o ignore trafBic signals;
o use their phones while walking or driving;
o ride without appropriate protective equipment;
o fail to use seat belts;
o speed or drive recklessly;
o walk or ride in unsafe areas;
o become distracted;
o fail to notice other road users.
Learning road safety helps learners develop habits that can protect themselves and others.
A simple decision such as stopping before crossing, wearing a seat belt, or wearing a properly
fitted helmet can make a very important difference.
Age-Appropriate Explanation for Learners
Think of the road as a place where many people must share space and cooperate.
Cars, motorcycles, bicycles, buses, pedestrians, and other road users all have different
movements and speeds. A vehicle may also take longer to stop than a person expects.
That is why learners should never assume:
“The driver will see me.”
Instead, develop the habit of:
Stop. Look. Listen. Think. Cross safely.
Remember:
Being careful is not being afraid of the road. Being careful means knowing how to use the
road safely.
2. Learning Objectives
At the end of the module, learners should be able to:
Objective 1: Identify common road hazards, traffic signs, signals, and situations that may
increase the risk of road crashes.
Objective 2: Demonstrate safe behaviors when walking, crossing roads, riding in vehicles,
cycling, or using other forms of transportation.
Objective 3: Apply appropriate decision-making strategies when faced with common road
and traffic safety situations.
Objective 4: Explain the importance of following traffic rules and using appropriate
protective measures to protect themselves and other road users.

Road safety begins with understanding that roads are shared spaces.
A safe road environment requires cooperation among:
🚶 Pedestrians
🚗 Drivers
🏍 Motorcycle riders
🚲 Cyclists
🚌 Public transportation users
🚦 Traffic authorities and road managers
Everyone has responsibilities.
For learners, the most important skills involve knowing how to:
walk safely;
cross roads safely;
recognize traffic signals;
understand basic road signs;
ride safely in vehicles;
use bicycles and similar devices responsibly;
avoid distractions;
respond appropriately to dangerous situations.
B. Who Is a Road User?
A road user is anyone who uses or interacts with the road.
This includes:
Pedestrians
People walking along or crossing a road.
Passengers
People traveling inside cars, buses, jeepneys, vans, or other vehicles.
Cyclists
People riding bicycles.
Motorcycle Riders
People operating motorcycles or riding as passengers.
Drivers
People operating motor vehicles.
Other Road Users
These may include people using mobility devices, delivery vehicles, public transportation,
and other forms of transportation.
Important Concept: Road safety requires everyone to pay attention to what is happening
around them.
C. Safe Walking
Walking may seem simple, but pedestrians can encounter many hazards.
Common pedestrian hazards
vehicles approaching from different directions;
vehicles reversing from driveways;
parked vehicles blocking visibility;
motorcycles passing between vehicles;
slippery surfaces;
construction areas;
poorly lit roads;
distracted pedestrians;
roads without sidewalks.
Safe Walking Habits
When walking:
✓ Use sidewalks or pedestrian paths when available.
✓ Walk in designated pedestrian areas.
✓ Stay alert to vehicles and other road users.
✓ Avoid using your phone while crossing.
✓ Avoid wearing headphones at a volume that prevents you from noticing your
surroundings.
✓ Follow pedestrian signals and traffic signs.
✓ Be particularly careful around driveways and intersections.
D. Crossing the Road Safely
Crossing a road requires attention and good judgment.
A useful strategy is:
STOP – LOOK – LISTEN – THINK – CROSS
🛑 STOP
Stop at a safe location before entering the roadway.
Do not suddenly step into the road.
👀 LOOK
Look for vehicles and other road users.
Look in different directions because vehicles may approach from more than one
direction.
👂 LISTEN
Listen for approaching vehicles, especially when visibility is limited.
Do not depend only on hearing because some vehicles may be quiet or
environmental noise may interfere.
🧠 THINK
Ask:
Is it safe to cross?
Do I have a clear view?
Is the traffic signal allowing me to cross?
Are vehicles actually stopping?
🚶 CROSS
Cross at an appropriate location and continue paying attention while crossing.
Do not assume that because one vehicle has stopped, every other vehicle has
stopped.
E. Pedestrian Crossings
A pedestrian crossing provides a designated place for people to cross.
However:
A pedestrian crossing does not mean you should stop paying attention.
Before crossing:
Stop.
Look for approaching traffic.
Check that vehicles are stopping or that the signal permits crossing.
Cross carefully.
Continue watching for vehicles.
Example
A learner reaches a pedestrian crossing.
A vehicle in one lane stops.
The learner should not automatically run across the road.
There may be another vehicle approaching in another lane.
Safety Lesson: Never assume that one stopped vehicle means the entire road is safe.
F. Traffic Signals
Traffic signals communicate instructions to road users.
🔴 Red
Generally means: STOP
Do not proceed when the signal prohibits movement.
🟡 Yellow / Amber
Generally means:
Prepare to stop / proceed only according to the applicable traffic signal and road
conditions.
A yellow light should not automatically be treated as a signal to speed up.
🟢 Green
Generally means:
Proceed when the movement is permitted and the way is clear.
A green signal does not eliminate the need to watch for other road users.
🚶 Pedestrian Signals
Pedestrian signals may indicate when crossing is permitted or when pedestrians
should wait.
Always follow the actual signal displayed.
G. Understanding Road Signs
Road signs communicate important information.
They may:
warn of hazards;
provide directions;
prohibit certain actions;
indicate regulations;
identify pedestrian areas;
provide information.
Common Examples
🚸 Pedestrian/School Crossing
Warns or informs road users that pedestrians may be crossing.
🛑 STOP
Requires the appropriate road user to stop.
🚫 NO ENTRY
Indicates that entry is prohibited.
⚠ WARNING SIGNS
Alert road users to potential hazards.
Interactive Question
What should you do when you see a traffic sign you do not understand?
Best response:
Slow down, observe the road environment, follow other applicable traffic controls, and ask
a responsible adult or qualified authority when appropriate.
