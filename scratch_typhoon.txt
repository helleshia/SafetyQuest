Module 5: Typhoon Safety
1. INTRODUCTION
Brief Introduction to the Topic
Typhoons are very powerful weather disturbances that can bring strong winds, heavy
rainfall, thunderstorms, flooding, landslides, storm surges, and other hazards. The
Philippines is particularly exposed to tropical cyclones because of its location in the
western North Pacific. Typhoons may develop quickly, change direction, or become
stronger, making preparation and continuous monitoring important.
Typhoon safety is not only about knowing what to do when a typhoon is already
happening. Preparedness before the storm, proper actions during the typhoon, and safe
recovery after the typhoon are all important parts of protecting oneself, family members,
classmates, neighbours, and the community.
For learners, typhoon safety means understanding weather warnings, preparing
emergency supplies, knowing where to go during evacuation, staying away from
dangerous areas, and following instructions from parents, teachers, local authorities, and
emergency responders.
Why the Topic Is Important
Knowing how to respond to a typhoon can help reduce injuries, prevent accidents, protect
property, and save lives. Many dangers associated with typhoons are not caused by
strong winds alone. Floodwater, electrical hazards, falling objects, landslides,
contaminated water, and storm surges can also pose serious risks.
Prepared learners are better able to recognize dangerous situations and respond
appropriately instead of panicking or making unsafe decisions. Even simple actions,
such as charging communication devices, preparing drinking water, staying indoors
when instructed, and avoiding floodwater, can make a significant difference.
Age-Appropriate Explanation for Learners
Think of a typhoon as a very large and powerful storm that can affect many places at
the same time. It may bring rain, strong winds, thunder, and sometimes flooding.
When a typhoon is approaching, your job is not to fight the storm or go outside to see
what is happening. Your job is to stay informed, prepare your emergency supplies, stay
with trusted adults, and follow safety instructions.
A simple way to remember typhoon safety is:
PREPARE → MONITOR → PROTECT → EVACUATE WHEN NEEDED → STAY
SAFE → RECOVER
2. Learning Objectives
At the end of the module, learners should be able to:
o Explain what a typhoon is and identify common hazards associated with
typhoons, including strong winds, heavy rainfall, flooding, landslides, and storm
surges.
o Identify appropriate preparedness measures before a typhoon, including
preparing an emergency kit, securing belongings, charging communication
devices, and monitoring official weather and emergency advisories.
o Demonstrate safe actions during and after a typhoon, particularly when dealing
with strong winds, flooding, electrical hazards, evacuation, and damaged
structures.
o Apply typhoon safety knowledge to realistic situations by choosing appropriate
actions during emergency scenarios and explaining why those actions help
protect people from harm.
3. Interactive Lesson
A. What Is a Typhoon?
A typhoon is a tropical cyclone that develops over warm ocean waters and
produces strong winds and heavy rainfall. Different regions use different terms
for tropical cyclones depending on where they occur. In the Philippines, the term
typhoon is commonly used for strong tropical cyclones.
A typhoon can affect communities in different ways. Some areas may experience strong
winds, while others may experience intense rainfall, flooding, landslides, or storm
surges.
Remember: One typhoon can create several hazards at the same time.
For example:
A typhoon produces heavy rain → rivers rise → flooding occurs → roads become
dangerous → electricity may be disrupted → evacuation may become necessary.
Therefore, learners should not focus only on the wind. Rainfall, floodwater, landslides,
and other hazards can also be life-threatening.
B. Common Hazards Associated with Typhoons
1. Strong Winds
Strong winds can damage roofs, trees, signs, windows, power lines, and other
structures.
Possible dangers include:
§ Falling branches
§ Flying objects
§ Broken windows
§ Damaged roofs
§ Falling electrical wires
§ Structural damage
Safety Action
Stay indoors when authorities advise people to remain inside. Keep away from
windows and glass doors, particularly when winds become strong.
2. Heavy Rainfall
Typhoons can produce large amounts of rainfall within a short period.
Heavy rainfall may result in:
Flash floods
River flooding
Overflowing drainage systems
Landslides
Road closures
Transportation disruptions
Example Situation
Situation: It has been raining heavily for several hours. You notice that water is
beginning to rise outside your house.
Safe response: Inform an adult, monitor official advisories, move important
belongings to safer locations if instructed and safe to do so, and prepare for
possible evacuation.
3. Flooding
Flooding occurs when water covers normally dry areas. Floodwater can be
dangerous even when it does not appear deep.
Floodwater may contain:
§ Sewage
§ Garbage
§ Sharp objects
§ Chemicals
§ Disease-causing microorganisms
§ Hidden holes or uneven surfaces
Floodwater can also hide electrical hazards.
Important Rule:
Never assume that floodwater is safe simply because it looks calm or shallow.
Avoid walking, swimming, playing, or driving through floodwater.
4. Storm Surge
A storm surge is an abnormal rise of seawater caused mainly by the winds and
atmospheric pressure associated with a tropical cyclone. Coastal communities
can be particularly vulnerable.
Storm surge can rapidly inundate coastal areas and may carry debris and other
dangerous materials.
If You Live in a Coastal or Low-Lying Area:
Follow evacuation instructions immediately when authorities advise evacuation
because of storm surge or other hazards.
5. Landslides
Heavy and prolonged rainfall can loosen soil and rocks, increasing the possibility
of landslides, particularly in steep or mountainous areas.
Warning signs may include:
• New cracks in the ground
• Falling rocks
• Unusual movement of soil
• Leaning trees or poles
• Sudden changes in streams
• Rumbling or unusual sounds from slopes
If authorities identify an area as dangerous, leave the area according to evacuation
instructions.
C. Understanding Weather and Emergency Warnings
Being safe during a typhoon requires more than simply knowing that a storm
exists. Learners should know how to receive and understand official information.
Monitor reliable sources such as:
PAGASA weather advisories
o Local government unit announcements
o Barangay announcements
o Official emergency responders
o School advisories
o Trusted news and emergency communication channels
Avoid relying solely on:
o Rumors
o Unverified social media posts
o Forwarded messages without sources
o Predictions from unofficial accounts
Interactive Question: Which information should you trust most during an
approaching typhoon?
A. A random social media post
B. An unverified group-chat message
C. An official weather advisory
D. A rumor from a neighbor
Correct Answer: C
Why? Official weather and emergency agencies provide information based on
monitoring and assessment. Information should still be checked for updates
because weather conditions can change.
D. Typhoon Preparedness: Before the Typhoon
Preparation should begin before dangerous weather conditions arrive.
1. Prepare an Emergency Kit
An emergency kit should contain essential items that may be needed during a
disaster.
Possible items include:
• Drinking water
• Ready-to-eat food
• Flashlight
• Extra batteries
• Battery-powered or hand-crank radio, if available
• First-aid supplies
• Necessary medicines
• Hygiene supplies
• Whistle
• Important contact information
• Power bank
• Extra clothing
• Blankets
• Basic tools, when appropriate
• Copies of important documents stored safely
The exact contents should be adjusted according to the family's needs.
